Case study

Vecini.app

Everything about the building in one place, instead of scrolling back through the group chat to find it.

Role  Product · UX · Development · QA · Deployment Built  July to September 2026 Status  Live
Problem

Buildings coordinate on WhatsApp, and WhatsApp loses things.

In Romanian apartment buildings the asociația de proprietari runs everything through a group chat. The hot water going off on Thursday, the lift stuck between floors, the vote on whether to repair the roof this year, all of it arrives in the same stream, in the same grey bubbles, and all of it has scrolled away by evening.

Announcements that matter get buried under chatter within a few hours. Problems get reported but never get a status, so the same broken intercom is reported four times by four people who each assume nobody has done anything about it. Decisions get made by whoever is still arguing at eleven at night, and afterwards there is no record of who actually agreed to what.

The neighbours who most need the announcement tend to be the older ones, the people who would have read a notice pinned to the avizier in the hall. They are also the least likely to scroll back three hundred messages looking for it.

My role

Everything except writing the code by hand.

I decided what the app does, what it deliberately leaves out, and what every screen says. I wrote the specifications in plain language, checked each change on my own phone, and cut features when they turned out to be worth less than they cost.

I tested the way a user would rather than the way a test suite does, by opening the app and noticing what felt wrong. That found things no automated test would have flagged:

  • The install prompt sat under the navigation bar. It rendered correctly and read well, and on a real screen the bottom third of it was covered.
  • Pinned announcements were pointless. Nothing ever un-pinned them, so a notice about last month's water outage stayed at the top forever. The fix was to make pinning expire on a date.
  • The loading bar flickered. It swept across and snapped back to zero every second and a half. That reads as the screen glitching rather than as progress.
  • "1 răspunsuri". Romanian counts in three forms rather than two, and the dashboard was getting the singular wrong on every discussion that had one reply.

I also did the parts nobody puts in a portfolio. Buying the domain, pointing DNS at the host, running database migrations against production, pasting the email templates in by hand.

What I built

The five things a building actually uses.

Announcements from the committee, where a priority notice expires on a date instead of staying up until somebody remembers to take it down. Issues with a real status (new, in progress, resolved), a photo, a location and a history of who changed what. Discussions for the things that are opinions rather than faults. Votes where the count is public and the ballot is anonymous. And a neighbour list where a phone number appears only if that person chose to share it.

Around those: joining by invitation code, three languages, push notifications, an archive so long lists stay readable, admin handover so a community is never left without one, and account deletion that anonymises rather than erases.

The product

What it looks like in use.

Product calls

The decisions that took longest were about what to leave out.

Deleting an account anonymises, it does not erase

Removing a departing neighbour's rows outright would tear holes through the building's own record, leaving replies that answer nobody and issues that nobody reported. Their name, email, phone number and access are deleted. What they wrote stays, attributed to a neighbour who left.

Priority announcements expire on a date

A flag that somebody has to remember to clear goes stale and quietly stops meaning anything, which is worse than not having one at all. Raising an announcement now means naming the day it stops mattering, and it comes down by itself.

Phone numbers are opt-in, and only for your own building

The number is optional and off by default, and it is visible only to people in the same community. That rule lives in the database rather than in the interface, so nothing that talks to the data directly can walk around it.

No feed, no likes, no infinite scroll

The app people are leaving already has all of those. Each section here has an end you can reach.

Tech & AI workflow

I wrote the specification and judged the result.

Every feature started as a sentence about what a neighbour should be able to do. I described the behaviour, looked at what came back on screen, and sent it round again when it was wrong. That is most of what product work has always been, with the typing taken out.

The parts that mattered most were the ones I sent back. A pinned announcement that never expires. A progress bar that lies about how far along it is. A plural form that is right for two of the three cases Romanian has. A passing test suite would not have caught any of them.
